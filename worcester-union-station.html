Union Station Area Plan

A master planning and implementation study organized around a central idea: use Union Station as an intermodal anchor while reshaping circulation, public space, and development parcels so public investment can unlock a more connected urban district.

LocationWorcester, Massachusetts
Date2001
Project typeMaster planning + urban design
Study focusTransit, circulation, redevelopment + implementation
Capabilities
Spatial DesignSystems & ImplementationVisual Communication

The big idea

The study area surrounds Union Station and extends from the Worcester Medical Center toward I-290, Pond Street, Green Street, and Worcester Center Boulevard. Although the immediate focus was north of Franklin Street, the plan deliberately considered how near-term infrastructure could preserve and strengthen future connections to the south.

01 · Transit

Make Union Station a true intermodal center

Increase transit use and pedestrian activity while accommodating regional and intercity buses, shuttles, taxis, and potential expanded passenger rail service.

02 · Development

Use infrastructure to create development value

Shape roadway and public-realm investments so they improve access while producing viable parcels for private redevelopment.

03 · Connections

Reconnect the station to surrounding districts

Strengthen pedestrian and vehicular links throughout the project area and maintain future circulation opportunities south of the station.

04 · Public realm

Build a more coherent urban setting

Use streetscape, open space, plazas, building placement, and pedestrian design to make the station district more attractive and legible.

Test circulation and development together

Rather than treating traffic engineering and redevelopment as separate exercises, the work was structured to compare alternative roadway systems by both transportation performance and the development opportunities they created. Alternatives considered parcelization, land use and building massing, bus and transit facilities, parking, streetscape, open space, and connections through Washington Square and to land south of Union Station.

Use the station as a development catalyst

The project connected transportation planning directly to urban design and real-estate feasibility. The study called for alternatives that simplified transfers between modes, improved pedestrian access to the station, leveraged existing public and private investment, and coordinated new development with the physical framework of streets, parcels, parking, and public space.

Move from plan concept to implementation

The scope extended beyond a preferred physical plan. It paired urban design with a phasing and financing strategy so redevelopment could be sequenced around infrastructure, property acquisition, parcel consolidation, transit operations, public funding, and permitting.

Preferred plan

Refine the physical framework

Develop a preferred site plan, land-use and design guidelines, illustrative graphics, and character studies that could guide later development proposals.

Phasing

Sequence enabling investments

Identify which roadway, property, open-space, and circulation improvements must precede development and which opportunities can move sooner.

Financing

Connect public investment to value creation

Estimate development investment, jobs, tax revenue, public funding opportunities, carrying costs, and alternative property-disposition strategies.

Permitting

Coordinate environmental approvals

Define a permitting strategy addressing MEPA review, transportation-agency participation, local approvals, and the additional studies needed to advance the plan.

Study framework

The work was conceived as an iterative process: document existing conditions and market potential; develop and compare alternatives; refine a preferred plan; then pair that plan with phasing, financing, transit, and permitting strategies. The supplied source material describes the study scope and intended deliverables rather than documenting final construction or implementation outcomes.
