Incremental Development

A phased sequence showing how individual redevelopment projects, strategic public investment, and the gradual completion of a connected street network can build out the South Frederick vision over time.

Infrastructure delivery categories

The sequence distinguishes between improvements that can be delivered with individual development and those that must wait for adjoining redevelopment, network continuity, or coordinated capital investment.

Frontage Construction Deferred

Frontage improvement postponed

Frontage improvement postponed until it can connect and function as part of the larger network.

Right-of-Way Dedicated / Construction Deferred

Secure the future street now

Future street land is secured now; construction occurs later when connections are available.

Existing Conditions

Phase 1 · Pioneer Development

Development logic: Begin with uncomplicated redevelopment sites while securing the future network and avoiding isolated pieces of infrastructure.

The first phase consists of the easiest and most immediately feasible redevelopment opportunities—the “low-hanging fruit.” These are generally sites where individual property owners can move forward without depending on large public infrastructure projects or coordinated redevelopment of multiple adjoining properties. Each pioneering project begins to establish the long-term street network shown on the Regulating Plan. Where planned streets cross or front a development site, the land necessary for those streets is dedicated as part of the development. Construction, however, is tied to whether the street segment can function at that point in time. Internal streets that can operate as part of the development's circulation system are constructed with the project. Where a street must eventually continue through an adjoining property that has not yet redeveloped, the connection is preserved but construction may be deferred. Where necessary, a temporary turnaround or other interim termination is provided. Likewise, frontage improvements may be deferred where constructing a short segment in isolation would not create a useful or coordinated improvement. The important point is that the planned right-of-way is secured from the beginning, even when physical construction must wait for adjoining redevelopment.

Phase 2 · Major Public Investment

Development logic: Use strategic public investment to unlock additional redevelopment opportunities and strengthen access to transit.

The second phase introduces a major public infrastructure investment intended to improve accessibility and help catalyze private redevelopment. A new overpass across I-270 connects the Ballenger Creek area with the Monocacy Station subdistrict. This establishes a significant new east-west connection and reduces the physical barrier created by the interstate. Unlike many of the smaller street segments being delivered incrementally by individual developments, the overpass is a major capital project requiring public coordination and funding. Its construction significantly changes the development potential of surrounding properties by creating better access to the Monocacy MARC station and connecting previously separated portions of the corridor. Private development continues concurrently, following the same dedication, construction, and deferral principles established in Phase 1.

Phase 3 · Transit-Oriented Redevelopment Accelerates

Development logic: Concentrate early major redevelopment around transit and large underutilized parking areas, while allowing the mall to begin transitioning incrementally from its transit-oriented edge.

With improved connectivity and growing market confidence, redevelopment begins to expand to larger properties, particularly sites characterized by extensive surface parking near the MARC station. Large parking-lot sites begin converting into more compact, mixed-use development. These projects construct the internal streets necessary to support their own circulation and establish walkable development blocks. Frontage streets or extensions that depend on neighboring properties may continue to be deferred. Likewise, major infrastructure elements that exceed the reasonable responsibility of an individual development project may remain deferred until sufficient redevelopment has occurred or public funding becomes available. This phase also marks the beginning of redevelopment of the mall property. Rather than redeveloping the entire mall at once, redevelopment begins on the portion of the site closest to the Monocacy MARC station, where transit access and emerging development activity make redevelopment most feasible.

Phase 4 · Expansion of Major Redevelopment Areas

Development logic: Extend the emerging urban framework outward from the earliest successful projects while maintaining flexibility for infrastructure that cannot yet be completed.

Redevelopment continues to spread outward from the areas that gained momentum in the previous phases. Additional portions of the mall property are redeveloped, extending the emerging street and block network deeper into the site. Planned rights-of-way continue to be dedicated as redevelopment occurs. Streets that can function independently are constructed, while segments dependent on adjoining redevelopment or larger infrastructure projects may remain subject to approved deferral arrangements. Another large surface-parking site near the MARC station is redeveloped, further strengthening the concentration of mixed-use development around transit. At the same time, a large undeveloped or underutilized office-area property near the Monocacy National Battlefield begins to develop. This represents an important expansion of redevelopment beyond the immediate transit station area and demonstrates that the emerging network can support a broader range of development locations and uses.

Phase 5 · Network Expansion and Spectrum Drive Realignment

Development logic: Use accumulated redevelopment and previously secured rights-of-way to complete larger network connections that could not reasonably be constructed during the earliest phases.

By this stage, redevelopment has progressed beyond the initial transit-oriented core and begins occurring more broadly throughout the planning area. Outlying properties redevelop incrementally, adding additional pieces of the planned street network and filling gaps between earlier projects. A major milestone in this phase is implementation of the planned realignment of Spectrum Drive. Because the realignment affects multiple properties and requires coordination beyond a single development site, it becomes feasible only after sufficient redevelopment has occurred and the necessary rights-of-way and connections have been assembled. The Spectrum Drive project improves continuity across the district and begins converting what had previously been a collection of disconnected development sites into a more coherent transportation network.

Phase 6 · Central Gap Redevelopment

Development logic: Once redevelopment momentum is established, fill the strategically important gaps between earlier projects and complete connections that previously could not function.

By Phase 6, redevelopment around the MARC station, the mall, and surrounding properties has created enough market momentum that more difficult or strategically located sites become viable. The central piece of land between the mall and the MARC station now redevelops. Earlier in the process, this property may have been constrained by uncertainty, fragmented access, or dependence on surrounding improvements. By this point, however, much of the surrounding street system and development framework is already established. Redevelopment of this central property closes a major gap in the urban fabric and links previously separate redevelopment areas into a continuous walkable district. Previously deferred street segments may also become constructible because adjoining properties and connecting streets are now in place.

Phase 7 · MD 85 Transformation and Major Regional Connections

Development logic: Once redevelopment has generated sufficient infrastructure contributions and network demand, combine private development obligations with major County and State capital investment to reconstruct the principal corridor and complete major regional connections.

Redevelopment now reaches the properties along the MD 85 frontage. As individual frontage properties redevelop, their required transportation obligations, deferred improvements, and applicable contributions collectively provide part of the funding necessary for a larger reconstruction of the corridor. Rather than requiring each individual property to construct disconnected pieces of MD 85, these resources can support a coordinated partnership between Frederick County and the Maryland State Highway Administration to reconstruct the corridor as a unified project. The resulting project can address roadway configuration, pedestrian and bicycle facilities, streetscape improvements, intersections, access management, and connections to the surrounding local street network in a coordinated manner. During this phase, the planned connection through to Reichs Ford Road also begins as a major County capital project. This establishes another important regional connection and further distributes traffic across the broader network rather than concentrating all movement on MD 85.

Phase 8 · Full Build-Out

Development logic: Complete the remaining gaps in the network and transition from a collection of incremental projects into the fully realized long-term urban framework.

The final phase represents long-term build-out of the South Frederick vision. Remaining redevelopment sites transition over time, completing the planned system of streets, development blocks, public spaces, and connections established by the Regulating Plan. Previously deferred street segments are constructed as adjoining properties redevelop and the necessary connections become available. Temporary turnarounds and interim circulation arrangements are removed as permanent streets are extended. The mall area has fully transitioned into an interconnected mixed-use district. The Monocacy MARC station is integrated into a broader transit-oriented neighborhood. MD 85 has evolved from an auto-oriented commercial corridor into a multimodal corridor connected to a fine-grained local street system. By this stage, what began as a series of isolated redevelopment projects has become a continuous network of walkable neighborhoods and mixed-use centers.
