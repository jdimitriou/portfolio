The basic idea

A coordinated implementation system.

The drafts divide implementation among several code chapters. The Comprehensive Plan and Regulating Plan establish the intended physical framework; zoning controls form and use; subdivision rules secure and deliver infrastructure; APFO addresses adequacy; and fee programs fund defined capital needs within legislatively designated areas.

Comprehensive Plan + Regulating Plan

Establish the planned growth framework, public Street and Road network, Development Areas, and other mapped form-based elements.

Chapter 1-13 · PDA

Creates a legislative geography for coordinated growth and for explicitly authorized alternative standards, procedures, mitigation, or funding tools.

Chapter 1-19 · Zoning

Applies FB/FBO form-based standards, Regulating Plans, Development Sites, frontage rules, mapped place types, and use regulations.

Chapter 1-16 · Subdivision

Turns the mapped framework into land control, public infrastructure, connected blocks, Transition Plans, and recordable obligations.

Chapter 1-20 · APFO

Coordinates adequacy review and provides the road-adequacy exception for development subject to the Mobility Fee.

Chapter 1-22 · Impact Fees

Creates Mobility Fee and Community Commons Fee programs within separately designated fee subareas inside PDAs.

Development Approval

Site plans, plats, infrastructure agreements, adequacy findings, fees, and later building permits implement the combined framework.

The package separates where growth is prioritized, what form development takes, how public infrastructure is secured, and how growth-related capital needs are funded while making those pieces work together.

Five chapters · five jobs

What each regulatory document does.

Each draft has a distinct function. The package works as a set of coordinated tools that activate at different stages of planning, development review, infrastructure delivery, and capital funding.

Chapter 1-13

Priority Development Areas

Creates PDAs entirely within mapped Growth Areas. A PDA is adopted, amended, or removed only by County Council bill and may serve as the geographic framework for specifically authorized alternative standards, procedures, mitigation, or funding mechanisms.

Not self-executing · No automatic rezoning · No automatic fee
Chapter 1-16

Subdivision + Infrastructure

Coordinates subdivision and site development with planned public infrastructure in FB/FBO areas. It introduces the Preliminary Development Layout Plan, Infrastructure Plat, Transition Plan, dedication/reservation tools, deferral agreements, and form-based Thoroughfare design rules.

Permanent framework · Land control · Phasing
Chapter 1-19

Form-Based Zoning

Establishes the FB and FBO districts and the physical rules governing Development Areas, Thoroughfares, Development Sites, building frontage, Site Frontage Zones, public space, allowed uses, and residential allocations.

Form first · Public realm · Walkable network
Chapter 1-20

Adequate Public Facilities

Coordinates APFO administration for form-based projects and establishes that development subject to the Mobility Fee is not subject to road adequacy requirements, while school, water, sewer, and other applicable adequacy requirements remain.

Road APFO exception · Other adequacy remains
Chapter 1-22

Development Impact Fees

Creates the Mobility Fee and Community Commons Fee, with fee-specific subareas, methodologies, credits, separate accounts, eligible capital improvements, and periodic review.

Proportionate share · Benefit relationship · Dedicated accounts
Geographic logic

Different maps answer different questions.

The regulatory layers are related but not interchangeable. A Growth Area establishes the broad comprehensive-planning envelope. A PDA is a legislative implementation geography inside a Growth Area. FB/FBO zoning and Regulating Plans govern form-based development. Fee subareas are separate overlays used only for the specific fee that created them.

1 · Community Growth AreaComprehensive Plan geography defining areas planned for growth.
2 · Priority Development AreaMust be entirely inside Growth Areas; adopted by County Council bill; does not itself rezone property or impose a fee.
3 · FB / FBO + Regulating PlanZoning geography that controls form, Development Areas, Thoroughfares, frontage, public-space relationships, and uses.
4 · Fee-Specific SubareaMobility Fee and Community Commons Fee apply only where their own subareas are expressly designated within a PDA.
Chapter 1-19

Form-based zoning controls the physical relationship between buildings, streets, and public space.

The zoning draft prioritizes development form over conventional use separation. Uses remain regulated, but the controlling logic is the shape of places: the mapped development context, the street hierarchy, the Development Site, and the edge between buildings and the public realm.

FB

Direct zone

The FB zone applies form-based standards directly to development, redevelopment, and land use.

FBO

Overlay zone

The FBO retains underlying zoning until the owner elects form-based standards or a trigger event activates them for a Development Site.

RP

Regulating Plan

Maps Development Areas, public Streets and Roads, Subdistricts, open spaces, landmark sites, buffers, and related form-based geography.

DS

Development Site

Defines the coherent area subject to review and includes the infrastructure and frontage necessary to make the current phase function within the larger plan.

⌂+

Introduction or expansion of residential use triggers form-based compliance, subject to stated exceptions for an ADU and certain conversions.

A principal use allowed by FB/FBO but not by underlying zoning triggers the form-based standards.

↗

An addition increasing gross floor area by more than 50% triggers compliance; additions extending toward a Thoroughfare comply regardless of size.

Chapter 1-16

Subdivision rules convert the mapped network into a buildable, recordable framework.

The subdivision draft is designed to keep planned Streets, Roads, blocks, access, and public infrastructure intact as development occurs incrementally, even where a site plan is proposed before a conventional subdivision.

Permanent framework

Preliminary Development Layout Plan

Used when a site plan proposes no new lots but planned Thoroughfares or public infrastructure affect the Development Site. It establishes the ultimate alignment, block structure, access, public-space and infrastructure framework and how the current phase relates to future development.

Chapters 1-20 + 1-22

APFO and impact fees are coordinated, but they do different things.

The Mobility Fee replaces project-specific road adequacy testing and road-APFO mitigation only where the fee actually applies. It does not replace safe-access, frontage, dedication, reservation, or other infrastructure obligations. The Community Commons Fee is separate and funds eligible parks, civic spaces, recreation, trails, and related public facilities.

Mobility Fee

Transportation capital

Applies only within a legislatively designated Mobility Fee Subarea inside a PDA and is based on the net increase in transportation demand.

  • Applies in lieu of road adequacy thresholds, testing, and road-APFO mitigation.
  • Can fund roads, street networks, pedestrian/bicycle facilities, transit-supportive improvements, signals, ITS, and related capital work.
  • Does not replace safe access, frontage, internal circulation, dedication, reservation, or required planned transportation facilities.
  • Credits are limited to eligible improvements that provide benefits beyond what is required solely to serve the project.
Community Commons Fee

Parks + civic facilities

Applies only within a legislatively designated Community Commons Fee Subarea inside a PDA and funds growth-related community-serving capital facilities.

  • Eligible facilities can include parks, plazas, civic greens, playgrounds, trails, greenways, recreation centers, and public amenities.
  • The fee does not replace on-site open-space, recreation, environmental, landscaping, or Regulating Plan requirements.
  • Existing deficiencies are excluded except to the extent an improvement adds new-development capacity, functionality, or public benefit.
  • Credits require eligible public benefit beyond facilities required solely to serve the development.
What remains under APFO?

Development subject to the Mobility Fee is exempt from the road adequacy requirements of Chapter 1-20, but remains subject to applicable school, water, sewerage, and other adequacy requirements. Form-based projects also remain subject to safe and adequate site access and the infrastructure obligations imposed through zoning and subdivision review.

Typical development logic

How a project moves through the framework.

The exact path depends on whether subdivision is proposed, whether FBO standards have been elected or triggered, what planned infrastructure affects the site, whether APFO applies, and whether the property lies in fee-specific subareas.

1
Read the maps

Confirm Growth Area, PDA, FB/FBO zoning, Regulating Plan, Development Area, Thoroughfares, and fee subareas.

2
Determine zoning path

For FBO property, determine whether form-based standards are optional, elected, or triggered for the Development Site.

3
Establish permanent layout

Use a preliminary plan when lots are created, or a Preliminary Development Layout Plan when a site plan must coordinate planned infrastructure without subdivision.

4
Design the site

Apply Development Area, frontage, building, parking, Thoroughfare, public-space, landscaping, use, and allocation standards.

5
Secure infrastructure

Record dedication, reservation, easements, Infrastructure Plats, Deferral Agreements, Transition Plans, and required security.

6
Resolve adequacy + fees

Complete applicable APFO findings and determine Mobility Fee / Community Commons Fee obligations and credits.

7
Approve + implement

Site plan / plat approval carries the permanent framework forward; impact fees are generally paid before the applicable building permit or zoning certificate.

Key terms

A short working glossary.

These terms are the connective tissue of the package. Understanding them makes the relationship among the five chapters much easier to follow.

Priority Development Area (PDA)
A County Council-established area located entirely within Growth Areas that can serve as a geographic framework for specifically authorized alternative implementation tools.
Regulating Plan
The mapped form-based zoning framework depicting and classifying Development Areas, public Streets and Roads, and other regulatory elements.
Development Site
The coherent area of a form-based project subject to site-plan review, including the development and infrastructure needed to make the phase function within the planned framework.
Preliminary Development Layout Plan
A County-approved permanent layout for site-plan development where no subdivision is proposed but planned infrastructure must be coordinated.
Infrastructure Plat
A recordable plat that creates no buildable lot and is used to secure land controls, easements, dedications, reservations, alignments, and related obligations.
Transition Plan
A plan for an interim condition when the permanent infrastructure framework will be implemented later; it defines how temporary conditions convert to the planned condition.
Deferral Agreement
A recorded agreement that allows specified infrastructure work to occur later under objective triggers, timing, security, maintenance, rights of entry, remedies, and successor obligations.
Site Frontage Zone
The street- or road-adjacent portion of a Development Site where principal buildings are placed to establish a defined pedestrian-oriented edge and meet qualifying frontage requirements.
Fee Subarea
A separately designated geographic area inside a PDA where a specific Mobility Fee or Community Commons Fee methodology applies.
